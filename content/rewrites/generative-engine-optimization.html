What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

A generative engine is a search tool that writes an answer instead of only listing links. It retrieves several sources, summarises them with a large language model, and usually cites some of them. ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode all work this way.

Generative engine optimization is the work of making your content one of those sources, and making sure the answer represents you accurately. The term was introduced in the paper GEO: Generative Engine Optimization by Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan and Ameet Deshpande, first published in November 2023 and accepted at KDD 2024, one of the main data science conferences.

In practice they overlap. GEO builds on SEO, because AI engines mostly retrieve pages that search engines can already find and trust. You don't replace SEO with GEO; you add a layer on top.

How AI engines choose which sources to cite

  1. Retrieve. The engine runs one or more searches behind the scenes and pulls candidate pages, so being indexed and findable still matters.
  2. Read and compare. The model reads several sources and looks for content that directly answers the question, with facts it can reuse.
  3. Write and cite. It writes an answer and attributes parts of it to sources. Content that is specific, well structured and easy to verify is easier to quote.

What the GEO research found

The researchers built a benchmark of 10,000 queries (GEO-bench) across 25 domains and tested nine ways of rewriting web content. They measured how much of each source made it into the AI answer, weighted by its position, plus a subjective impression score.

Three more findings matter for businesses:

  • Smaller sites benefit most. Citing sources raised visibility by 115.1% for pages ranked fifth in search results, while the top-ranked page's visibility fell by 30.3% on average. GEO is one of the few levers where a smaller company can outmanoeuvre a bigger one.
  • It depends on the topic. Statistics helped most in law and government topics; quotations helped most in people and society, explanation and history topics.
  • Combinations work best. Fluency Optimization plus Statistics Addition beat any single method by more than 5.5%.

What Google says about AI Overviews and AI Mode

Google's own guidance is short. According to Google Search Central, you can apply "the same foundational SEO best practices for AI features as you do for Google Search overall". To be shown as a link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, "a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet". There are "no additional technical requirements".

Two practical consequences: a page blocked from indexing or from snippets can't be cited, and traffic from AI features is reported inside the normal Search Console Performance report under the "Web" search type, not separately.

How to do GEO: a 10-step checklist

This is the process I use with clients, built on the research above and on what we see in real AI answers.

  1. Get the SEO basics right. Pages must be crawlable, indexed and allowed to show snippets. If you block AI search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot (ChatGPT search) or PerplexityBot in robots.txt, those engines can't cite you.
  2. Find the prompts that matter. List the questions buyers ask AI tools in your category, not only the keywords they type into Google.
  3. Answer first. Put a direct, two or three sentence answer at the top of each page, then the detail.
  4. Add evidence. Cite credible sources, add specific statistics with dates, and quote named experts. These were the strongest methods in the research.
  5. Write plainly. Short sentences, defined terms, one idea per paragraph. Readability alone improved visibility by 15 to 30%.
  6. Structure for extraction. Clear headings, tables for comparisons, lists for steps, and FAQs with real questions.
  7. Make your entity clear. Consistent company, product and author information across your site, schema markup, LinkedIn, Crunchbase and directories, so AI describes you correctly.
  8. Show expertise. Named authors with real credentials, reviewers for sensitive topics, and dates that show content is current.
  9. Earn third-party mentions. AI engines often cite reviews, comparisons, news and forums. Digital PR, reviews and expert commentary put your name in the sources they trust.
  10. Measure and repeat. Track citations and mentions monthly, update pages that lose ground, and add evidence where competitors are cited instead of you.

Common GEO mistakes

  • Treating GEO as a replacement for SEO. If search engines can't find and trust the page, AI engines rarely will.
  • Keyword stuffing. It didn't work in the research and it reads badly to people.
  • Unsourced claims. Numbers without a source are less useful to an AI that needs to verify what it says.
  • Ignoring off-site sources. For many prompts, AI cites reviews, comparisons and forums rather than vendor sites.
  • Measuring only clicks. Many AI answers don't produce a click. Track mentions and citations too.
